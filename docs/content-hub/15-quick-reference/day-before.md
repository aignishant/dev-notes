# Day-Before Checklist

Print this. Do it in order. Gives you the calm, prepared state that gets jobs.

## 72 Hours Before

- [ ] Re-read [Cheat Sheet](cheat-sheet.md) once
- [ ] Re-read your 6-8 STAR stories from [STAR Stories Bank](../13-leadership/star-stories.md); replace template details with your real ones
- [ ] Run `mkdocs serve` on this prep site; skim section indexes for anything you've forgotten
- [ ] Research the company's latest announcements, blog posts, recent product launches

## 48 Hours Before

- [ ] Review Section 11 ([Advanced](../11-advanced/index.md)) — OAuth, caching, rate limiting, error handling
- [ ] Review Section 12 ([System Design](../12-system-design/index.md)) — scaling, multi-tenant, migration
- [ ] Re-read [Red-Flag Answers](red-flags.md) — know what not to say
- [ ] Practice one whiteboard problem from [Scenarios](../14-scenarios/whiteboard.md) out loud, timed
- [ ] Confirm logistics (time zone, video platform, dial-in, start time)

## 24 Hours Before

- [ ] Re-read your STAR stories ONE final time — don't memorize, know the beats
- [ ] Review [Glossary](glossary.md) for any term you're foggy on
- [ ] Prepare 3-5 questions to ask your interviewers — tailored to each person if you know who
- [ ] Lay out clothes / setup the interview space / charge laptop
- [ ] Set backup alarm; confirm calendar entry
- [ ] **Light dinner, good sleep** — no heroic cramming tonight

## Morning Of

- [ ] Breakfast — protein + slow carbs, not just caffeine
- [ ] Drink water; avoid caffeine overdose (makes you talk fast)
- [ ] 10-minute walk or no-phone quiet time
- [ ] Arrive (physically or online) 10 min early
- [ ] Have this reference sheet handy (see **"What to Have on Your Desk"** below)

## What to Have on Your Desk

Physical or digital — your call, but visible:

- [ ] **One-page cheat sheet** (print the core defs + data flow from [Cheat Sheet](cheat-sheet.md))
- [ ] **Your STAR stories** — title + 2-sentence outline per story, NOT full scripts
- [ ] **Your questions for them** — written out
- [ ] **Names + titles of interviewers** (if you know them) — helps you personalize questions
- [ ] **Water** — plain, room temperature
- [ ] **Paper + pen** — for whiteboarding or note-taking

Do NOT have:

- Multi-page scripts (you'll sound read-aloud)
- Your CV (you wrote it; you know it)
- Phone notifications visible
- Anything you'll be tempted to check mid-interview

## The Opening Move

Interviewer says "so tell me about yourself." Answer in **90 seconds** with this structure:

1. **Current role + domain** (10s) — *"I'm a senior engineer leading the Google SecOps Content Hub team..."*
2. **5-year arc** (30s) — started doing X, grew into Y, now leading Z
3. **Recent notable win** (30s) — a specific project with a specific outcome
4. **Why this role** (20s) — honest reason for looking

Practice this until it's smooth. The opening sets tone for the whole loop.

## Mid-Interview Resets

If you lose the plot mid-answer:

1. **Stop.** Silence is fine.
2. **Re-anchor.** *"Let me restate the question..."*
3. **Structure out loud.** *"I'll cover three things: X, Y, Z."*
4. **Continue.**

Much better than rambling yourself into a corner.

## If You Don't Know Something

Don't bluff. Use one of these:

- *"I don't know that specific detail. Here's how I'd find out: ..."*
- *"I haven't worked with that directly, but it's adjacent to X which I do know..."*
- *"That's actually a gap for me. What's the right framing?"*

Admitting the gap + showing how you'd close it is a lead-signal.

## Closing

When asked "any questions for us?":

1. **Ask your prepared questions** — ask the best ones first in case time runs out
2. **Ask a follow-up to something the interviewer said** — shows you were listening
3. **Never ask about salary, benefits, or time off in the first interview** — save for recruiter/later stages

Ending move:

> *"Thanks for the time. This was a great conversation — I'm definitely interested. What are next steps?"*

Direct, positive, moves the process forward.

## Immediately After

- [ ] Jot down the interviewer's name + one thing they said (for follow-up thank-you)
- [ ] Walk 10 minutes, eat something, hydrate
- [ ] Send thank-you email within 24 hours — short, personalized, mentions one specific thing from the conversation
- [ ] Log what was asked in your interview journal — helps for future loops

## Mindset Reset

Interviews aren't exams. They're **two-way professional conversations** where both sides are evaluating fit. You're interviewing them, too.

If this interview doesn't go well: a single interview is never worth what it feels like in the moment. You've led a team for 5 years. That's not erased by one awkward answer.

If it goes well: celebrate briefly, then prepare for the next round the same way.

## Good Luck

You've built a lot over 5 years. This site and your real experience are more than enough. The rest is executing calmly on what you already know.
