# 📆 3-month fast-track

> 12 weeks. ~2,000 problems. 3–4 hours per day. The most popular plan in this bible — and the one with the highest reported success rate.

---

## Who this plan is for

✅ You have **3 months** until your target interview window.
✅ You can do **3–4 hours per day** consistently (fits around full-time work or a heavy course load).
✅ You want **depth + coverage + sustainability** — not a sprint, not a marathon.

This is the plan most successful candidates use. It's long enough to build real intuition, short enough to maintain urgency, paced for sustainability over 90 days.

---

## The bargain this plan makes

| Buy | Cost |
|---|---|
| All 20 patterns mastered (not just covered) | 90 days of consistency |
| 2,000 problems with deep retention | Some advanced topics still light |

## Daily rhythm

**Working professional:**
```
06:30 — wake, exercise
07:30 — block 1 (1 hr): morning re-do (2 problems from memory)
09:00–17:00 — work
18:00 — dinner + walk
20:00 — block 2 (2 hrs): topic + 3 problems
22:00 — log progress
22:30 — sleep
```

**Student/full-time:**
```
08:00 — wake, breakfast
09:00 — block 1 (2 hrs): topic chapter + 3 problems
11:00 — break (1 hr)
12:00 — block 2 (1.5 hrs): 2 problems
13:30 — lunch + walk
14:30 — class / personal time
20:00 — block 3 (1 hr): 1 problem + log
```

Weekends: 5 hours each. Saturday for advanced topics + mock; Sunday for system design or rest.

## Phase-by-phase (3 months = 12 weeks = 4 phases)

### Phase 1 — Foundations + linear DS (Weeks 1–3)

**Goal:** unbreakable foundations. Arrays, strings, hash, linked lists.

- **Week 1:** Python (full Foundations section), Big-O, recursion thinking, dry-run method
- **Week 2:** Arrays — basics, two pointers, sliding window, prefix sums, Kadane (read each subtopic + 5 problems = 25 problems)
- **Week 3:** Strings, hash maps, linked lists basics + 25 problems

**Mock #1** end of week 3 — pure DSA, easy/medium.

### Phase 2 — Trees, graphs, recursion (Weeks 4–6)

**Goal:** master the recursion-heavy topics. This is where most people plateau.

- **Week 4:** Stacks, queues, heaps + 25 problems
- **Week 5:** Trees (DFS, BFS, BST, advanced patterns) + 30 problems
- **Week 6:** Graphs (BFS, DFS, topo sort, components, Dijkstra/MST intro) + 30 problems

**Mock #2** end of week 4. **Mock #3** end of week 6.

### Phase 3 — Patterns + DP + advanced (Weeks 7–9)

**Goal:** see every problem through the lens of one of the 20 patterns.

- **Week 7:** Backtracking (subsets, permutations, combinations) + recursion deep-dive + 25 problems
- **Week 8:** DP — 1D + 2D + LCS family + knapsack family + 35 problems
- **Week 9:** Binary search variants + intervals + greedy + bit manipulation + 30 problems

**Mock #4** week 7. **Mock #5** week 8. **Mock #6** week 9.

### Phase 4 — System design + polish + behavioral (Weeks 10–12)

**Goal:** convert "prepared" into "peaked." Add the system design and behavioral pillars.

- **Week 10:**
  - System design fundamentals (databases, caching, message queues, microservices)
  - 1 Tier-1 design project (URL shortener, end-to-end)
  - Continue 15 DSA problems
- **Week 11:**
  - 2 more Tier-1 design projects (Twitter feed, YouTube/Netflix)
  - Behavioral STAR templates
  - 8 personal stories prepared
  - 15 DSA problems
- **Week 12:**
  - Mock interview week — 6 mocks across 7 days (DSA, system design, behavioral)
  - 2 days of light review
  - Last day off

**Mocks #7–#12** in week 12.

## Self-check tests at week boundaries

At end of each week, pick **4 fresh problems** in the week's topic that you haven't seen. Solve in 30 min each.

- 4/4 → ahead, raise difficulty next week
- 3/4 → on track
- 2/4 → spend weekend re-grinding the week
- ≤1/4 → don't move on; spend extra week consolidating before phase advance

## What changes vs the 6-week plan

The 6-week plan is the same shape but compressed. The 3-month plan adds:

1. **Repetition cycles** — every 4 weeks, a "re-do week" of ~50 previously-solved problems from memory (this is what builds long-term retention)
2. **Advanced topics** (tries, segment trees skimmed, DSU) that the 6-week plan can only touch
3. **3 system design projects** vs 1 in 6-week
4. **Larger behavioral bank** (8 vs 5 stories)
5. **Slack for life** — sick days, family, work crunches

That last one matters most. The 3-month plan succeeds because it survives reality.

---

## Final 2 weeks

Week 11 = polish + behavioral.
Week 12 = mock-heavy + sleep-heavy.

Do not add new content in weeks 11–12. Anything you don't know by end of week 10 is not going to land in your long-term memory in 2 weeks; better to peak on what you do know.

4. If you didn't — you now know exactly what to work on. Pick **5-week balanced** for round 2.
