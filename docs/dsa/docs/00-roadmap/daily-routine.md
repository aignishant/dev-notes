# Daily routine

> What an actual study day looks like. Steal this template.

---

## The basic rhythm — 3 blocks

Every productive study day has the same shape, no matter the length:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[🌅 Warm-up<br/>10–20% of time] --> B[🔥 Core work<br/>60–70% of time]
    B --> C[🌙 Wrap-up<br/>10–20% of time]
```

- **Warm-up** — re-do 1–2 problems from memory. Cheap reps. Wakes the brain.
- **Core** — new content + new problems. The actual study.
- **Wrap-up** — log progress, mark what was hard, plan tomorrow's first 10 minutes.

That's it. Don't overcomplicate.

---

## Sample 2-hour day (working professional, after work)

```
20:00 — Warm-up (15 min): re-solve yesterday's hardest problem from memory
20:15 — Core (1 hr 30 min):
        - Read today's topic chapter (30 min)
        - Solve 2 medium problems (1 hr)
20:00 [actually 21:45] — Wrap-up (15 min):
        - Log: which problem was hardest? Why?
        - Mark tomorrow's first problem
22:00 — Off
```

This is the most common rhythm. Sustainable indefinitely.

---

## Sample 4-hour day (weekends, full-time student)

```
09:00 — Warm-up (30 min): 2 re-do problems
09:30 — Core block 1 (1 hr 30 min): topic read + 3 problems
11:00 — Break (30 min): walk, snack
11:30 — Core block 2 (1 hr 30 min): 3 more problems
13:00 — Wrap-up (30 min): log, plan tomorrow
13:30 — Off (rest is real work)
```

Total 5 problems in core (you'll hit ~6 with re-do). Highly productive.

---

## Sample 8-hour day (full-time prep)

```
07:00 — Wake, exercise (30 min), breakfast
08:00 — Warm-up (1 hr): morning re-do — 2 problems from memory + journal
09:00 — Core block 1 (2 hrs): topic + 3 problems
11:00 — Break (1 hr)
12:00 — Core block 2 (2 hrs): 4 problems
14:00 — Lunch + walk (1 hr) — non-negotiable
15:00 — Core block 3 (2 hrs): 3 problems + 1 timed (no peeking)
17:00 — Wrap-up (30 min): log, snack
18:00 — Off / dinner / rest of evening
22:00 — Sleep
```

You'll hit 8–10 problems on a good day. **8 hours is the ceiling** — past 10 problems/day, retention falls off a cliff. Quality > quantity.

## The 25-min Pomodoro structure (inside any block)

For each 25 minutes of "core" work:

```
0:00 → Read or attempt
22:00 → Stop, however far you got
22:00–25:00 → Reflect: what's the next concrete step?
25:00–30:00 → Stand, walk, water, stretch
30:00 → Next pomodoro
```

After 4 pomodoros (≈2 hours), take a 30-minute break.

This is real. Without it, hour 3 onward is mostly unconscious staring at a screen.

---

## What to do FIRST when you sit down

> 🥇 **Always**: re-do yesterday's hardest problem from memory.

Why: starts you with a win. Builds the habit of active recall. Catches forgetting before it sets in.

If you can't re-do it: that's the most useful information of your whole day. Don't move on. Re-read the solution, re-derive the insight, try again.

---

## What to do LAST before you stop

> 🥇 **Always**: write 3 lines.
>
> 1. "Today I learned: ___"
> 2. "Today I struggled with: ___"
> 3. "Tomorrow I will start with: ___"

The last one is the most important. Tomorrow-you starting a session at "where do I begin?" loses 20 minutes. Tomorrow-you starting at "I will begin with X" loses zero.

## The "I'm tired" backup routine

Some days you have nothing. Don't skip — do this minimum instead:

```
20 min — 1 easy problem from memory (yesterday's, last week's, anything)
10 min — read 1 page of any chapter you've covered
00 min — log: "Today was a low-energy day. I did the minimum. That counts."
```

30 minutes. **Skipping a day is much worse than doing the minimum.** Habits compound; broken streaks compound the wrong way.

## Active recall vs passive reading

> 📖 **Reading is not studying.**

Reading feels productive but it isn't. The test: close the book, can you reproduce the idea, the code, the complexity?

Convert reading to active recall:

| Passive (low value) | Active (high value) |
|---|---|
| Re-read solution | Cover the screen, re-derive the solution |
| Highlight code | Copy code from memory, run it, fix bugs |
| Watch video at 2× | Pause every 60 seconds, predict what happens next |
| Skim chapter | After each section, write 3 bullets summarizing it |
| Flip through cheatsheet | Cover the answer, recite the answer, then check |

Aim for **80% active, 20% passive**. Most people do the opposite and wonder why nothing sticks.

## Sleep, food, exercise — non-optional

This isn't a wellness lecture, it's an algorithm efficiency note.

- **Sleep < 7 hours → you'll forget half of yesterday by tonight.** Period. Doesn't matter how long you study.
- **Skipping meals → blood sugar crashes mid-block → bad reps imprinted.** Eat regular meals.
- **Zero exercise → mental fog by week 2.** 30-min walk per day, minimum.

If your study schedule conflicts with sleep, **the study schedule loses**. There's no plan in this bible that beats consistent sleep.

## The weekly review (Sunday or Monday)

Every week, 30 minutes:

1. Count problems solved this week. Trend up? Flat? Down?
2. Score self-check test (4 fresh problems from this week's topic).
3. Identify weakest topic.
4. Plan next week's first 3 days.
5. Review your "stuck" list — re-attempt 1 problem from it.

This is when course corrections happen. Skip the review = drift.

## Common rhythm anti-patterns

> 🚫 **Marathon Saturdays.** "I'll skip weekdays and do 10 hours Saturday." Doesn't work — you forget the marathon by Wednesday.
>
> 🚫 **All easy / no medium.** Easy problems train *typing*, not *thinking*. Mix difficulty within each block.
>
> 🚫 **All medium / no recall.** New problems with zero re-do = study amnesia.
>
> 🚫 **Cramming the night before.** Sleep > one extra problem. Always.
>
> 🚫 **No log.** Without a log, you can't see trends, can't notice burnout, can't celebrate progress. Keep one.

## What progress feels like

- **Week 1–2:** Most things hard. Slow.
- **Week 3–4:** A few patterns click. Surprisingly fast on those.
- **Week 5–8:** Lots of "I've seen this before" moments. Confidence builds.
- **Week 9–12:** You're noticing patterns *before* the problem statement ends.
- **Final week:** Most problems feel familiar. Hard ones still hard, but you have a process.

If your trajectory matches this roughly, you're on the right track. If you're stuck at "everything hard" past week 6, **change something** — most likely you're not doing active recall and re-do.
