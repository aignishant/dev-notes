# PSU interview style

> Formal, panel-based, suit-and-tie. ISRO / DRDO / BARC / GATE-route PSUs grade composure, project depth, general awareness — and the smallest etiquette breaches matter.

<span class="phase-status phase-done">Phase 14 — PSU interview etiquette + content</span>

## 🏛️ Two routes, same tone

| Route | Companies | Selection |
|---|---|---|
| **GATE-based** | BHEL, NTPC, ONGC, IOCL, GAIL, NHAI, HPCL, BPCL, GSPL, Power Grid, NHPC | GATE score → DV → GD → PI |
| **Own exam** | ISRO ICRB, DRDO CEPTAM/RAC, BARC OCES/DGFS, ECIL, BEL, HAL | Subject test → PI |
| **Banking / regulator** | RBI Grade B, SEBI Grade A | Prelims → mains → PI |

Tone is uniform: **formal, panel of 3-7 interviewers, government / boardroom setting**. No "let me whiteboard" — these are conversation rounds.

---

## 🎯 What PSU panels grade

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Subject mastery** | Textbook-deep. They want depth, not breadth. |
| **Project ownership** | One project, 5+ minutes of detail, ready diagrams. |
| **General awareness** | Current affairs, your home state, the company itself. |
| **Composure** | Do you stay calm under stress questions? Do you maintain eye contact across a panel? |
| **Clarity of language** | English (often Hindi optional) — clear, slow, correct. |
| **Ethical sense** | RBI / SEBI specifically grade integrity scenarios. |

## 🎩 Etiquette — the smallest things matter

### Before entry

- Wait outside the room until called by name.
- **Knock twice, wait for "come in"**, then enter.
- Walk to the chair. **Do not sit until invited**.
- Greet the panel — "Good morning, Sir / Madam" — making brief eye contact with each.

### During the interview

- **Sit upright.** Back straight, feet flat on floor, hands on thigh / table.
- **Address the speaker.** When one panellist asks, focus on them; glance briefly at others while answering.
- **"Sir" / "Madam"** every answer until told otherwise.
- **Slow down.** Speed = nervousness signal.
- **Don't interrupt.** Even when you know what they're about to ask.
- **Don't argue with corrections.** "You're right, sir, I had it wrong" wins more points than defending.

### Stress questions

PSU panels **deliberately** ask edge-of-knowledge questions to see how you handle not knowing. Two graceful exits:

1. **"I don't know, sir. May I attempt to reason it out?"** → then attempt.
2. **"I haven't studied that specific topic, sir. I do know [related topic]; should I share that?"**

Faking an answer is the failure mode they're testing for.

### Exit

- Wait for the panel to indicate the interview is over.
- Stand, **say "Thank you, sir / madam"**, push your chair back gently, walk out without turning your back too sharply.

## 📋 The four content buckets

### 1. Self introduction (2-3 minutes max)

Standard, but PSU-flavored. Add **a sentence on why this PSU specifically**.

> "Good morning, sir / madam. I'm [Name], from [Town, State], pursuing [Degree] from [Institute] with a [CGPA / Rank]. My core academic interests are [domain — e.g., 'communication systems and signal processing']. My final-year project, [Title], applied [technique] to [problem]. I've also interned at [if applicable]. Outside academics, I [club / hobby / sport]. I'm interested in joining [PSU] specifically because [genuine reason — sector mission, scale, location, R&D culture]."

### 2. Project deep-dive (15-20 minutes possible)

This is where most PSU interviews live. Prepare **one** project deeply.

| Layer | Be ready to answer |
|---|---|
| **Why this problem?** | Real motivation, not "professor assigned it". |
| **Block diagram** | Be able to redraw on a whiteboard if asked. |
| **Each block — what it does** | One sentence per block, plus the math / data flow if probed. |
| **Tools used** | Specific software, libraries, hardware. Defendable choices. |
| **Your exact role** | If team project, name your scope clearly. |
| **Failures + fixes** | What went wrong, how you debugged, what you'd do differently. |
| **Result with numbers** | Even rough numbers beat qualitative claims. |
| **Future scope** | One concrete next step. |

**Trap**: claiming credit for teammates' work. Multiple panels include subject experts; they'll find inconsistencies.

### 3. Subject + fundamentals

PSU panels probe core syllabus from your branch. They ask **textbook** questions — not LeetCode, not industry frameworks.

For CS / IT, drill: OS / DBMS / Networks / DSA basics / OOP / TOC. Coverage is the same as the [PSU consolidated page](../07-popular-problems/psu/index.md).

**Format**:

- "What is paging?" → 30-second textbook definition + 1 example.
- "Difference between TCP and UDP?" → table of 4-5 differences + when each is used.
- "What is normalisation?" → definitions of 1NF / 2NF / 3NF / BCNF + a worked decomposition.

**Style**: speak as if reciting from the textbook chapter, not improvising. Panel expects clean canned answers; improvisation reads as ungrounded.

### 4. General awareness + current affairs

Topics they probe:

- **Your home state / city.** Who's the CM? Major industries? Recent news? Population (rough)?
- **The PSU itself.** Founded year, current CMD, key projects in news, headquarters, scale.
- **The sector.** "What's happening in the oil sector right now?" / "What's NTPC's stance on renewables?" — be ready.
- **Current affairs.** 2-3 sector-relevant items from the last 30 days.
- **Your branch's recent news.** "What was [recent ISRO mission]'s objective?"

**Source**: 30 days of *The Hindu* + PIB highlights + the PSU's own annual report intro is enough.

## 🪤 PSU-specific traps

??? warning "The 'I don't read newspapers' answer"

    Direct disqualifier in many PSU panels. Even if true, never say it. Have **2-3 current affairs items** ready to volunteer if asked.

??? warning "The over-prepared resume answer"

    Reciting a memorised speech for "tell me about yourself" reads worse than a slightly-rough natural one. Memorise the **structure**, not the script.

??? warning "Fake CGPA / project / internship"

    Document verification catches almost everything. Panels also detect fakeness via probing; getting caught here ends the candidacy permanently with that org.

??? warning "Casual address"

    Calling an interviewer by name, or "you" without "sir/madam", reads as disrespect at the PSU panel. The norms are stricter than corporate.

??? warning "Negative views about India / government"

    PSU = government. Even mild negative views ("our country's R&D is decades behind") read as bad fit. Frame any criticism as constructive: "I think there's room to grow in X area".

## 💡 Specific etiquette by PSU

| PSU | Quirk |
|---|---|
| **ISRO ICRB** | Heavy on subject; panel often includes scientists from your discipline. Project must defend its physics / math foundations. |
| **DRDO CEPTAM** | Defence-flavored; expect questions on indigenisation, defence R&D, your willingness to work in non-metro labs. |
| **BARC OCES / DGFS** | Most rigorous of all — multi-stage. Subject + research aptitude. Be ready for graduate-level depth. |
| **RBI Grade B** | Adds finance / banking / economics layers. Read RBI's annual report cover to cover. |
| **SEBI Grade A** | Securities / regulatory focus. Read recent SEBI orders + market events. |
| **NTPC / NHPC / Power Grid** | Energy-sector questions: thermal vs renewable mix, grid stability, distribution losses. |
| **GAIL / IOCL / ONGC / HPCL / BPCL** | Oil & gas mix questions, pipeline / refinery basics, current crude prices. |
| **HAL / BEL / ECIL** | Defence-electronics / aerospace questions, indigenous projects. |
| **BHEL / NHAI / RVUNL** | Heavy industry / infra; sector + scheme awareness (Bharatmala, Sagarmala etc.). |

## ⏱️ Typical panel structure

| Phase | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 2-3 min | Self intro + warm-up question. |
| Project | 10-15 min | Deep-dive with one or two interviewers leading. |
| Subject | 10-15 min | Round-robin technical questions. |
| Awareness / HR | 5-10 min | Current affairs + "why us" + situational. |
| Closing | 1-2 min | Any questions from your side; thank-you. |

Total: 30-45 minutes typical, longer for ISRO / BARC / RBI Grade B.

## ➡️ Practice prompt

Schedule **one mock interview** with a friend / mentor playing the panel role. Wear your formal clothes. Sit at a desk. Have them ask:

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Walk us through your project.
3. Three subject questions from your branch.
4. One current-affairs question.
5. One stress question ("What if your project doesn't work in production?").
6. "Do you have any questions for us?"

Record audio. Listen back. The first listen is uncomfortable; the second is where you find the fixes.
