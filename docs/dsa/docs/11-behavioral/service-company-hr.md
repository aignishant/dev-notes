# Service company HR rounds

> The rounds that reject more candidates than the technical rounds.

<span class="phase-status phase-done">Phase 14 — service HR playbook</span>

---

## 🏛️ The reality

For **TCS / Infosys / Wipro / HCL / Cognizant / Tech Mahindra / Capgemini / Accenture / IBM India / LTIMindtree / Mphasis / DXC**, the HR round is a hard gate. Technically competent candidates routinely fail this round. The grader is checking **fit** — and "fit" has specific meaning here that engineering-focused candidates often miss.

(Zoho's HR round is closer to product-company style; the rest of this page may not apply.)

---

## 🎯 What service HR really grades

| Signal | What they're checking |
|---|---|
| **Communication** | Spoken English fluency. Confidence without arrogance. |
| **Willingness to relocate** | Will you go to Bangalore / Hyderabad / Pune / Chennai / Kolkata / wherever the project lands? |
| **Willingness to work shifts** | US shift, UK shift, on-call rotations. |
| **Bond acceptance** | Will you stay 2 years? They've lost too many to product-company poaching. |
| **Domain flexibility** | Will you take Java when you applied for Python? Mainframe when you wanted cloud? |
| **Cultural fit + composure** | Can you be put in front of a client? Will you embarrass the company? |

If all six of these are clear-yes, you pass. If any is unclear, you fail.

## ✅ How to nail the basics

### Greeting + body language

- **Stand up** when interviewer enters (in-person).
- **Wait for "please sit"** before sitting.
- **Eye contact.** Steady, not staring.
- **Hands on table or in lap.** Not in pockets.
- **Smile naturally.** Forced smile reads worse than no smile.

### Dress code

- **Men**: formal shirt (light colour, ironed) + dark trousers + closed shoes + clean shave or trimmed beard.
- **Women**: formal salwar / saree / business suit + minimal jewellery.
- **Both**: hair tied back / neat. ID card or roll number visible.

This is not a nitpick — graders form impression in the first 30 seconds.

### Speaking

- **Slow down.** Nervous candidates speed up; English clarity drops; grader's confidence drops.
- **Pause before answering.** A 2-second pause reads as thoughtful, not clueless.
- **One thought at a time.** Don't string 3 ideas with "and… and… and…"
- **No filler.** "Like", "basically", "actually" used >2 times in an answer = ding.
- **Acknowledge then answer.** "Sure ma'am, I'll talk about that. I think…" rather than launching cold.

## 📋 The questions you WILL be asked

Have answers prepared for every one. Each should be 60-90 seconds spoken.

??? question "1. Tell me about yourself."

    **Template**:

    > "Good morning, ma'am / sir. I'm [Name], pursuing / completed [Degree] from [College]. My core technical interests are [2 areas], demonstrated through [project / internship]. Outside academics, I'm involved in [club / sport / volunteer]. I'm looking forward to [growth at company / domain exposure / professional environment]."

    **Trap**: reading your résumé aloud. Pick highlights, don't recite.

??? question "2. Why do you want to join us?"

    **Template**:

    > "Three reasons. First, the scale — [Company] works with [scale, e.g., 'Fortune 500 clients across 50+ countries']. Second, the [specific training program — TCS ILP, Infosys Mysore, Wipro WASE, etc.]. Third, the chance to work on [specific domain that matches your interest]."

    **Trap**: "stable job + good salary". Honest, kills the round.

??? question "3. Are you willing to relocate?"

    **Template**:

    > "Yes, ma'am, anywhere in India. I understand [Company] has offices in [list 3-4]. I'm flexible."

    If you're genuinely not flexible (e.g., aging parents): say so honestly with reason. Lying gets caught at the offer stage and everyone is unhappy.

??? question "4. Are you willing to work shifts?"

    **Template**:

    > "Yes — I understand client time-zone alignment may need US shift / UK shift. I'm comfortable with that."

??? question "5. Are you OK with the bond?"

    **Template**:

    > "Yes, I've read the terms. I understand it's a 2-year service agreement with [₹X penalty if I leave early]. I'm committed for that period."

    **Trap**: trying to negotiate down the bond in HR round. It's non-negotiable; pushing back fails the round.

??? question "6. Tell me about your project."

    **Template**: STAR-light.

    > "I built [Project] which solves [problem]. My role was [specific scope]. I used [tech stack]. Key challenge was [specific]; I solved it by [specific]. Outcome: [specific metric or qualitative result]."

    **Trap**: claiming everyone else's work. Be honest about your scope.

??? question "7. Strengths and weaknesses."

    **Strengths**: pick 2, with one example each. "I'm a fast learner — when I needed to learn React for my project, I was building components in a week."

    **Weakness**: a real one with active mitigation. "I sometimes over-commit; I've started using a personal tracker to estimate task time before agreeing."

    **Traps**:
    - Three strengths, no examples.
    - "My weakness is perfectionism / hard work / caring too much" → universally panned.

??? question "8. Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"

    **Template**:

    > "I'd like to be a [specific role — Senior Engineer, Tech Lead, Domain Expert] at [Company], having grown deep in [chosen domain]. I see [Company]'s career path enabling that."

    **Trap**: "I want to start my own company / do MBA / go abroad." Disqualifying.

??? question "9. What do you know about our company?"

    Founded year, headquarters, current CEO, employee count (rough), revenue (rough), recent news (one item from past 30 days), key services / verticals.

    **Trap**: making up facts. They'll catch it.

??? question "10. Do you have any questions?"

    **Always have one**. Three safe genres:

    1. "Could you tell me more about the training program / first-year journey?"
    2. "What does a typical day for an entry-level engineer look like?"
    3. "What kinds of projects do new hires get assigned to?"

    **Trap**: "What's the salary?" / "When do you announce results?" / "I have no questions."

## 🪤 The traps that fail otherwise-strong candidates

??? warning "The mismatched register"

    Speaking too casually ("yeah", "yo", "kind of", "uh") in HR round = ding. They want you in front of clients; clients are formal.

??? warning "The unfocused background"

    "I like everything — coding, designing, testing, management, …" — reads as no real direction. Pick 2 things and own them.

??? warning "The negative talk"

    Bad-mouthing your college, professors, classmates, current company, anyone — disqualifying. Even mild. They're imagining you talking about THEM next year.

??? warning "The over-confident close"

    Saying "I'll definitely accept any offer" is weaker than "I'd love the opportunity to work here, and if selected I'd be ready to join in [date]". Confidence > desperation.

??? warning "The phone-call slip"

    HR sometimes calls before the formal round. Same rules apply. "Hi" / "Yo" / answering while eating = ding.

## 🌍 Communication signal — how it's actually graded

For roles with client-facing potential (most of them):

- **Spoken English clarity.** Not accent — clarity. Slow + correct beats fast + wrong.
- **Paragraph structure when speaking.** "First… second… third…" or "I'd like to mention X, then Y, then Z" wins.
- **Active voice.** "I built X" beats "X was built by me".
- **Confidence in answers.** "I'm not sure, but my best guess is…" beats silent stalling.
- **Recovering from mistakes.** If you misspeak, calmly correct: "Sorry, I meant Y, not X."

---

## ⏱️ Length

Most HR answers should be **45-75 seconds**. Going over 90 seconds reads as rambling.

Open with a one-line preview ("Three reasons…") and the interviewer can stop you if they want.

## ➡️ One day before

1. Sleep 8 hours.
2. Lay out clothes the night before.
3. Print 2 copies of your résumé. Bring a pen.
4. Practice the 10 questions out loud. Time yourself.
5. Read 30 minutes of *The Hindu* or *Indian Express* for current affairs. PIB highlights are gold.

---

## ➡️ One hour before

1. Arrive 30 minutes early to the venue (or 15 minutes for online). Buffer for traffic / login issues.
2. Hydrate. Don't eat heavy.
3. Switch phone to silent.
4. Last skim of your résumé — be ready for questions on EVERY line of it.

You'll do fine.
