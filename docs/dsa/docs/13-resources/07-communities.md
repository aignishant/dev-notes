# Communities

> Where to find study buddies, mock partners, and people who'll answer your questions when you're stuck. Solo prep is a 50-percenter; community prep is the rest.

<span class="phase-status phase-done">Phase 14 — Resources</span>

---

## Why community matters

The compounding effects:

- **Mock partners** force you onto a schedule. "I have a mock at 7pm on Thursday" is unfalsifiable accountability.
- **Conversations expose blind spots**. You'll discover patterns you've been quietly missing for weeks.
- **Referrals**. Most FAANG-tier offers come through referrals, not cold applications.
- **Emotional bandwidth**. Job-hunting is grindy. People in the same boat help.

## Subreddits

### Active and useful

- **r/cscareerquestions** — broad career advice. High noise, occasional gold.
- **r/leetcode** — problem-specific discussion, weekly thread for new problems.
- **r/ExperiencedDevs** — senior+ perspective. Less interview-focused; more "is this normal?"
- **r/SystemDesign** — small but quality.
- **r/csMajors** — university-level; skip if you're past graduation.
- **r/IndianStreetBets / r/developersIndia** — for India-specific job-hunting context.

### What to filter

Most subreddits are 80% complaining + 20% useful. Filter for posts with:
- Specific company names + actual interview experiences
- Compensation discussions (helpful for negotiation)
- "How I prepped" retrospectives with concrete schedules

Skip:
- "Should I leave my job?"
- "Is X market still hiring?"
- "Rate my resume" (use a service for this)

## How to actually use community

The trap: joining 5 Discords + 3 Slacks + 4 subreddits + watching 8 YouTubers, and confusing consumption with progress.

The protocol:

1. **One Discord. One Slack. One subreddit. One YouTube channel.** That's enough.
2. **Show up, don't lurk.** Post your weekly progress. Ask the dumb questions. People help people who try.
3. **Two mock partners on rotation.** Schedule weekly. Hold them to it.
4. **One mentor or accountability partner.** Someone 1-2 levels above you who'll review your trajectory monthly.
5. **Time-box community time** to 2-3 hours/week. The rest of the time is solo grinding.

## Asking for help — getting good answers

The questions that get answered are the ones that show effort. Compare:

> ❌ "How do I prepare for FAANG?"

vs.

> ✅ "I've solved 200 LeetCode mediums and read DDIA. My system design rounds keep failing on the deep-dive section — interviewers say I don't go deep enough on schema. Anyone got a drill that fixed this for them?"

The second gets answers. The first gets ignored.

Template:

```
Context: [where you are, what you've done]
Specific problem: [what's blocking you]
What you've tried: [list]
What you're asking for: [advice / mock partner / resource]
```

## Giving back

Once you have an offer, return the favour:

- Post your prep retrospective on the same forums you used.
- Mock-interview people in earlier stages.
- Offer to review resumes / system design walk-throughs.
- Share what your interviewers asked (within NDA limits) so others can prep.

The community runs on this loop. If you're getting value from it now, you owe back later. Don't be the person who leaves the moment they get their offer.

---

## Red flags — communities to avoid

- **"Pay $X for the secret list of FAANG questions."** It doesn't exist. They're scammy.
- **Discord groups that focus on "leaks" of interview questions.** Borderline ethical, won't help long-term, sometimes gets you flagged.
- **Forums that disparage specific companies / interviewers by name.** Gossip-quality; signal-to-noise is awful.
- **Bootcamp-style groups asking $thousands for "guaranteed FAANG."** No bootcamp can guarantee anything.

The good stuff is free. Pay only for: 1:1 mentorship, paid mocks, and structured courses (Educative.io, AlgoExpert, Designgurus) — and only when you've maxed out free resources.

## 🃏 Cheatsheet

- One Discord, one Slack, one subreddit, one YouTuber. Don't sprawl.
- Show up actively in communities; don't lurk.
- Two recurring mock partners; weekly cadence.
- One mentor or accountability partner.
- Time-box community to 2-3 hr/week.
- Ask high-effort questions; you'll get high-effort answers.
- Give back after you land. The loop runs on it.
