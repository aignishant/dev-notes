# 🇺🇸 US Government Cyber Careers

> The agencies that wrote the playbook. NSA, CISA, FBI Cyber, USCYBERCOM, DOE national labs — these are the most consequential cybersecurity jobs in the world. Hard to get, slow to onboard, life-shaping once you're in.

This chapter is a tactical guide: which agencies do what, how their hiring actually works, what the clearance process is, and how to actually land a role.

## The major employers

### NSA — National Security Agency

Mission: signals intelligence collection (SIGINT) + information assurance (IA). Located at Ft. Meade, MD.

**Cyber-relevant directorates / divisions**:
- **Cybersecurity Directorate (CSD)** — created 2019; defensive mission, runs CESER and Cybersecurity Collaboration Center
- **Tailored Access Operations (TAO) / Computer Network Operations (CNO)** — offensive operations
- **Research Directorate** — long-horizon technical research
- **GenCyber, NCAE-Cyber-Operations** — academic outreach

**Typical roles**:
- Computer scientist (CNE/CNA developer, tool developer)
- Vulnerability analyst / vulnerability researcher
- Reverse engineer
- Cryptanalyst
- Mathematician (cryptographer)
- Intelligence analyst

**Pay**: GS scale + DC locality + Cyber Excepted Service (CES) flexibility. Senior tech roles can pay well above standard GS via CES.

**Clearance**: TS/SCI minimum. Often with full-scope or counterintelligence polygraph.

**Pathways**:
- [Stokes Educational Scholarship](https://www.intelligencecareers.gov/nsa/students-internships) — undergrad, full ride + summer work, then 1.5x years service per year of scholarship
- [DCWA (Director's Cyber Workforce Apprenticeship)](https://www.intelligencecareers.gov/nsa/students-internships) — 2-year apprentice → full role
- **Cooperative Education / Internship Program** — paid summer + full-time conversion
- **Civilian Hire** via [intelligencecareers.gov](https://www.intelligencecareers.gov/nsa) and USAJobs

### CISA — Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency

Mission: defend US critical infrastructure (federal civilian, state/local/tribal, election, ICS). Headquarters Arlington, VA. DHS subordinate.

**Cyber-relevant divisions**:
- **Cybersecurity Division (CSD)** — vulnerability management, monitoring, incident response, threat hunting
- **Stakeholder Engagement Division** — JCDC (Joint Cyber Defense Collaborative)
- **Infrastructure Security Division** — ICS-CERT, sector engagement
- **National Risk Management Center**

**Typical roles**:
- Threat hunter
- Incident responder (ICS-CERT, Hunt and Incident Response Teams "HIRTs")
- Vulnerability analyst / coordinator
- Cybersecurity advisor (regional, work with sector partners)
- Detection engineer
- Penetration tester (some red team work, smaller than NSA's)
- Cyber defense analyst

**Pay**: CISA uses **Cybersecurity Talent Management System (CTMS)** — bypasses standard GS. Flexible pay bands, faster hiring, market rates.

**Clearance**: Often Secret minimum; TS/SCI for sensitive teams.

**Pathways**:
- [CISA Cyber Career Pathways Tool](https://niccs.cisa.gov/workforce-development/cyber-career-pathways-tool) — explore work roles
- [CISA Apply Today (CTMS roles)](https://www.cisa.gov/careers) — apply directly
- **Cyber Talent Management System (CTMS)** announcements — outside USAJobs, faster
- USAJobs for non-CTMS roles
- **Pathways internship → conversion** track

### FBI — Cyber Division

Mission: investigate cyber crime + nation-state intrusions affecting US victims. HQ: J. Edgar Hoover Building, DC. Field offices nationwide.

**Cyber-relevant entities**:
- **Cyber Division** at HQ
- **Cyber Action Team (CAT)** — fly-out IR for major incidents
- **NCIJTF (National Cyber Investigative Joint Task Force)** — interagency
- **IC3 (Internet Crime Complaint Center)** — public-facing intake

**Typical roles**:
- **Special Agent (cyber-track)** — sworn law enforcement, GS-1811. Trained at Quantico (~22 weeks). Cyber Specialty Skill.
- **Computer Scientist** (GS-1550) — non-sworn technical analyst, no academy
- **Intelligence Analyst** (GS-0132)
- **Forensic Examiner** (CART — Computer Analysis Response Team)
- **Tactical Specialty Officer (TSO)** — physical/tech ops support

**Pay**: Special Agents start at GS-10 step 1 + LEAP (~25% law-enforcement availability pay) + locality. Computer Scientists at GS-9 to GS-13 starting depending on experience.

**Clearance**: Top Secret + SCI as needed.

**Pathways**:
- [FBI Jobs](https://www.fbijobs.gov/) — agent applications, computer scientist roles, internships
- **Honors Internship Program** — 10-week paid summer
- **Collegiate Hire Program** for recent grads
- **Cyber Talent Network** — ongoing pipeline

Special Agent prereqs are strict: US citizenship, 4-year degree, 23–37 age window (with waivers for certain backgrounds — military, prior LE), drug history limits, debt limits, fitness test. Computer Scientist track is more flexible.

### USCYBERCOM — US Cyber Command

Mission: military cyber operations. Sub-unified combatant command under STRATCOM, dual-hatted with NSA Director. HQ: Ft. Meade.

**Components**:
- **Cyber National Mission Force (CNMF)** — defend the nation
- **Cyber Combat Mission Forces (CCMF)** — supporting combatant commands
- **Cyber Protection Forces (CPF)** — defend DoD networks

**Typical roles**:
- **Military officers / enlisted** — assigned via service cyber branches:
  - **Army Cyber Branch (17-series)** — enlisted 17C cyber operations specialist; officer 17A
  - **Air Force / Space Force Cyber** — 1B4X1, 17X enlisted; 17S officer
  - **Navy Cryptologic Warfare** — CTN/CTI/CTM/CTR ratings
  - **Marine Corps Cyberspace Officer** — 17XX
- **Civilian DoD employees** under Cyber Excepted Service (CES)
- **Contractors** (Booz Allen, Leidos, GDIT, ManTech, MITRE, etc.) — major footprint

**Pay**: Military pay tables + cyber retention bonuses (significant). Civilian via CES.

**Clearance**: TS/SCI minimum.

**Pathways**:
- Direct enlistment into a cyber rating/MOS
- ROTC + cyber branch selection
- Service academies (USMA, USAFA, USNA)
- DoD CES civilian via [Cyber Excepted Service](https://dodcio.defense.gov/Cyber-Workforce/CES/)
- Contractor → conversion (very common)
- **Cyber Direct Commissioning** — recently established programs across services for direct-from-civilian commission as cyber officer

### DoD Components — non-USCYBERCOM

Beyond USCYBERCOM, DoD has many cyber missions:

- **DC3 (Defense Cyber Crime Center)** — forensics, training, vulnerability disclosure
- **DCSA (Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency)** — cleared industry oversight + clearance investigations
- **DTRA, DARPA** — research and counter-WMD cyber
- **NCIS, OSI, CID** — service investigative arms
- **Service Components** — Army Cyber Command (ARCYBER), Air Forces Cyber (AFCYBER), Marine Corps Forces Cyberspace Command (MARFORCYBER), US Fleet Cyber Command (FCC), Coast Guard Cyber Command (CGCYBER)
- **DISA (Defense Information Systems Agency)** — DoDIN ops, CSSP

### DOE National Laboratories

The often-overlooked elite. The 17 national labs run by DOE host extraordinary cyber and ICS research.

| Lab | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Idaho National Laboratory (INL)** | ICS / OT cyber; CyberCore Integration Center |
| **Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL)** | Cyber analytics, ICS, AI security |
| **Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL)** | Nuclear, HPC, cyber, AI safety |
| **Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL)** | National security cyber, advanced research |
| **Sandia National Laboratories** | Cyber for national security, ICS, embedded |
| **Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL)** | HPC, cyber AI |
| **Argonne, Brookhaven, NREL, others** | Various |

**Typical roles**: research scientist, cyber engineer, ICS engineer, postdoc.

**Pay**: Lab pay scales — competitive with industry. Often higher than agency federal pay.

**Clearance**: Q-clearance (DOE equivalent of TS) for most cyber roles. Some labs have public-trust positions for unclassified work.

**Pathways**:
- Direct application via lab career sites
- Internships ([SCGSR](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/scgsr) for grad students; **CyberCorps SFS placement** valid here)
- **NNSA Graduate Fellowship Program (NGFP)**
- **Operational Technology Defender Fellowship** at INL

### Other federal cyber employers

- **Treasury / OFAC / FinCEN** — financial cyber investigations
- **Secret Service** — financial fraud + network intrusion (esp. financial sector)
- **State Department / DSS** — diplomatic security, often technical roles
- **Department of Energy / FERC** — energy sector regulatory cyber
- **NRC (Nuclear Regulatory Commission)** — nuclear sector cyber
- **NIST** — standards (800-53, CSF, NICE Framework)
- **CFTC, SEC** — financial sector cyber regulators
- **VA, IRS, USDA, etc.** — every cabinet department has cyber teams under their CIO/CISO
- **GSA / 18F / TTS** — civic tech with security focus
- **USDS** — temporary tour of duty model, embeds technologists

### Federal contractors

The reality: most "federal cyber jobs" are actually contractor positions. Federal jobs are slower to hire and pay less; contractors are faster, often pay more, and many roles are functionally identical to civilian-federal work.

**Top federal cyber contractors**:
- Booz Allen Hamilton, Leidos, SAIC, GDIT, ManTech, CACI
- MITRE, Aerospace, IDA (federally funded research and development centers — FFRDCs)
- Mandiant (now Google), CrowdStrike Services, Palo Alto Unit 42 — IR contracts
- Smaller specialty shops: Two Six Technologies, Margin Research, Trail of Bits (some), Praetorian (some)
- IT primes: Accenture Federal, IBM Federal, Northrop Grumman, BAE, RTX/Raytheon

**Pros**: faster onboarding, often better pay, civilian work environment.
**Cons**: subject to contract recompete cycles, less mission ownership, sometimes 2-tier with the government employees.

Contracting is the most common back-door into cleared federal cyber work.

## How federal hiring actually works

### USAJobs

[USAJobs.gov](https://www.usajobs.gov/) is the front door for most federal civilian roles. Reality:

- Each posting has a **closing date** that may be sudden — apply within days of posting
- Each posting has a **questionnaire** — be honest but don't undersell. The system filters by self-rating
- Resumes can be **3–6 pages** — opposite of industry. Include hours/week, supervisor name, every duty
- Use **Resume Builder** within USAJobs the first time; it forces the right format
- **Veterans' preference** is real and meaningful; if you have it, list it
- **Pathways** (interns, recent grads, fellows) is a separate hiring authority — much easier path in

### Special hiring authorities

These bypass the standard GS process and are how most cyber hiring happens now:

- **Cybersecurity Talent Management System (CTMS)** — CISA, DOD getting expanded use. Faster, market-rate pay.
- **Cyber Excepted Service (CES)** — DoD-wide. Flexible pay/grades.
- **Direct Hire Authority (DHA)** — for hard-to-fill cyber roles. Can hire on-the-spot at career fairs.
- **Schedule A** — disability-based hiring authority.
- **Pathways** — interns, recent grads, presidential management fellows.
- **Veterans' preference + VRA** — Veterans Recruitment Appointment.
- **Schedule B** — Peace Corps / VISTA / similar.
- **CyberCorps Scholarship for Service** — full ride + 3-year federal service commitment. Stipend ~$25–37k/year + tuition. Apply via [sfs.opm.gov](https://sfs.opm.gov/).
- **DoD Cyber Scholarship Program (CySP)** — similar to SFS but DoD-focused.

### Federal resume format

The federal resume is its own art form:

```
NAME
contact, citizenship, veterans pref, clearance status

WORK EXPERIENCE
Title — Employer
Address (city, state) | Dates (MM/YYYY – MM/YYYY) | Hours/week: 40
Salary: $XX,XXX/yr | Supervisor: Jane Doe — phone (may we contact?)
- Bullet describing duty 1, with quantified outcome
- Bullet describing duty 2, with KSA mapping
[5–10 bullets per role]

EDUCATION
Degree — Institution — City, State
GPA, Honors, Year (or expected)

CERTIFICATIONS
Cert — Year — Provider

ADDITIONAL INFORMATION
- Languages, tools, frameworks
- Publications, talks
- References available on request
```

Match the **specialized experience** language from the job announcement directly. The HR specialist scoring your resume is matching keywords.

## Security clearances — the long pole

### Clearance levels

| Level | Investigation | Renewal | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public Trust** (low/mod/high risk) | Not technically a clearance; suitability check | 5y | Weeks to months |
| **Confidential** | T3 (Tier 3) investigation | 15y | 3–6 months |
| **Secret** | T3 | 10y | 3–6 months (varies wildly) |
| **Top Secret** | T5 (Tier 5, formerly SSBI) | 6y | 6–18 months |
| **TS/SCI** | T5 + adjudication for SCI access | 6y | 9–24 months |
| **TS/SCI w/ poly** | Above + counterintelligence (CI) or full-scope (FS) polygraph | 6y | 12–24 months+ |
| **Q (DOE)** | Equivalent to TS, separate adjudication | 5y | 6–18 months |
| **L (DOE)** | Equivalent to Secret | 10y | 3–6 months |

### The SF-86

[Standard Form 86](https://www.opm.gov/forms/pdf_fill/sf86.pdf), aka eQIP — the application. ~120 pages of detail. You list:

- Every residence for the last 10 years (addresses, dates, neighbors who can verify)
- Every job for the last 10 years (with reference contacts)
- Every foreign contact (people, not businesses) you've had close contact with in the last 7 years
- Every foreign travel in the last 7 years
- Every drug use, ever (the form asks)
- Financial issues — debts, bankruptcies, defaults
- Mental health (mental health treatment is no longer a per-se issue; concealment is)
- Every relative — names, citizenships, addresses
- Every alias, social media account, email address

**Lying is the killer.** Drug history, financial trouble, foreign contacts — most are mitigable. Concealment of any of these on the SF-86 is the fastest way to lose a clearance forever.

### Common disqualifiers (and how to navigate them)

Whole books exist on clearance adjudication. The most common areas of concern (per [Adjudicative Guidelines, 2017](https://www.dni.gov/files/NCSC/documents/Regulations/SEAD-4-Adjudicative-Guidelines-U.pdf)):

- **Financial issues** — pay your debts, file taxes, manage credit. Bankruptcy ≠ disqualifying if recent and explained.
- **Foreign influence** — close family abroad, foreign business interests. Mitigable if disclosed and analyzed.
- **Personal conduct / honesty** — lies on the form, infidelity that creates leverage, criminal history. Disclose.
- **Drug involvement** — past use is mitigable (depending on substance, recency, frequency); current use is disqualifying. Marijuana is **still federally illegal** for clearance purposes regardless of state law.
- **Sexual behavior** — only matters if it creates leverage (extramarital, illegal, etc.) or non-disclosure
- **Alcohol** — pattern of issues (DUIs, treatment) matters
- **Cyber security** (yes, applied to you) — unauthorized access, illegal cyber activity. CTFs and bug bounties on authorized scope are fine; unauthorized is not.
- **Allegiance to the US** — acts indicating disloyalty. Rare disqualifier.

### Polygraph

Some IC roles require **counterintelligence (CI) polygraph** or **full-scope (FS) polygraph**. Topics:

- **CI**: foreign contact, espionage, terrorism, unauthorized disclosure
- **FS**: CI topics + lifestyle topics (drug use, criminal activity, falsifications)

Polygraph is controversial; many strong candidates fail. Tips:
- Tell the truth in the pre-test interview — surprises during test are catastrophic
- Don't try to "beat" it (countermeasures are detectable and disqualifying)
- It's a multi-session process; one inconclusive session isn't a final result
- Stay calm; the test is uncomfortable for everyone

### Reciprocity and portability

A clearance held with one agency *transfers* to another with much less re-investigation, IF kept current and sponsorship continues. Key constraints:

- Clearance is **sponsored**; if you leave, you have ~24 months before it lapses
- Going from contractor to government (or vice versa) is straightforward
- Going DoD → IC sometimes requires re-adjudication for SCI eligibility

## Citizenship requirements

- US citizenship is required for nearly all cleared roles
- For some IC roles: **birth in the US** preferred but not always required (depends on agency, role)
- Naturalized citizens: clear, but with extra investigation for foreign contacts

## Pay reality

Public GS pay tables: [opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/salaries-wages](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/salaries-wages/). For 2026 (typical):

| Grade | Base | DC locality |
|---|---|---|
| GS-9 | $59k | $77k |
| GS-11 | $72k | $93k |
| GS-12 | $86k | $112k |
| GS-13 | $103k | $134k |
| GS-14 | $122k | $158k |
| GS-15 | $144k | $187k |

CES / CTMS roles can exceed these caps significantly. Defense contractor pay for equivalent roles is typically 10–30% higher than federal civilian.

Compensation is rarely competitive with FAANG. The mission and impact are. Choose accordingly.

## Realistic timelines

- **Apply to first interview**: 4–12 weeks
- **First interview to verbal offer**: 2–8 weeks
- **Verbal offer to formal offer**: 2–8 weeks
- **Formal offer to start (clean clearance already held)**: 2–6 weeks
- **Formal offer to start (need new clearance)**: 6–24 months
- **Start to first day actually working on cleared materials**: same day or up to a few weeks (read-on)

The clearance wait is the hardest part. Use the time well — get certs, build portfolio, take lower-clearance interim positions if offered.

## Strategies for breaking in

### Path 1 — Direct civilian hire

Apply on USAJobs and CTMS. Slow. Need persistence.

### Path 2 — Contractor first

Most accessible. Apply to Booz, Leidos, GDIT, MITRE, Mandiant Federal, etc. They'll often sponsor a clearance for the right candidate. Once cleared and seasoned, lateral move to federal civilian or another contractor is easier.

### Path 3 — Military

Enlist or commission into a cyber-track service role. 4–6 years of paid training + experience + clearance + post-service GI Bill + lifelong network. Hardest path but most-respected outcome for many.

### Path 4 — Scholarship

CyberCorps SFS — full ride + paid summer at federal agency + 3-year federal service commitment after graduation. Best deal in cyber education for those who qualify. Apply through partner universities.

### Path 5 — Internship to conversion

NSA's, CISA's, FBI's, DOE labs' internships. Convert at 70–90% rate if performance is good. Best path for current students.

### Path 6 — Lateral from industry

After 5+ years of strong industry experience + relevant certs (OSCP, CISSP), agencies actively recruit. Particularly common for AppSec, RE, threat hunting roles.

## Networking events that matter

- **DEF CON** (Las Vegas, August) — federal recruiting heavy in DEF CON Career area
- **Black Hat USA** (Las Vegas, July/August) — different vibe; corporate-cleared crowd
- **BSides DC** — federal-heavy crowd
- **AFCEA TechNet Cyber** (Baltimore, May/June) — DoD-heavy
- **RSA Conference** (San Francisco, April/May) — broadly mixed
- **ICS Cybersecurity Conference / S4** — ICS-specialized
- **Special Communications Electronics Symposium (SCES)** — Ft. Meade-area, IC-flavored
- **SANS conferences** — most have federal recruiters present
- **(ISC)² Capital Region chapter** — DC-area networking
- **DEF CON Groups (DC###)** — local communities, low-pressure networking

Events also matter if you're not yet cleared — recruiters can extend conditional offers contingent on clearance.

## What helps you stand out

In rough order of impact:

1. **A current TS/SCI clearance** — opens 80% of doors instantly. If you have one, list it prominently.
2. **Published research** — CVE write-ups, blog posts, conference talks
3. **OSCP + one of OSEP/OSWE/OSED** — for offensive roles
4. **CISSP** — for senior or management roles
5. **GitHub portfolio** — visible, real tools and writeups
6. **CTF profile** — CTFtime team rank, HTB / THM rank
7. **Internships at any of the agencies** — even one summer
8. **Veterans' preference** — if you have it
9. **A degree from an [NSA-designated CAE-CO or CAE-CD school](https://www.caecommunity.org/cae-map)** — good signal
10. **Languages** — Mandarin, Russian, Arabic, Korean, Farsi can move you to specific teams

## Interview questions

1. *"Why government? Why this agency specifically?"*
2. *"What's your eligibility status? Any complications on the SF-86?"*
3. *"Walk me through a recent technical project."*
4. *"How would you handle classified materials before having full read-on?"*
5. *"Why this role over a private-sector equivalent paying 2x?"*
6. *"Walk me through your understanding of NIST 800-53 / RMF / FedRAMP."* (compliance roles)
7. *"What's your favorite recent vulnerability writeup, and what would you have done differently as the defender?"*
